## Abstract

This proposal defines how ethics concerns in psychedelic and mental health communities are adjudicated: what authority an adjudication body holds and what it explicitly does not, how concerns are categorized and escalated on a defined clock, who learns what and when, and how the body is constituted and held accountable.

It does not prescribe a single process every community must run. It proposes conditions any process should be able to demonstrate it met, so that fairness becomes verifiable rather than asserted.

## Motivation

Communities in this field handle ethics concerns with improvised processes. Existing models do not fit: professional associations carry built-in conflicts because practitioners judge peers they depend on; regulatory bodies are slow, punitive, and designed for legal violations rather than relational harm; for-profit complaint platforms are incentivised to amplify rather than resolve. The default is social media exposure, rumour, and community pile-on, which produces neither fairness nor safety.

The cases that have surfaced publicly demonstrate that the absence of a transparent process protects no one. It protects institutions briefly and then fails badly when a pattern becomes visible. The recurring problem is not individual violations; it is the absence of any credible process a community can point to as evidence that accountability happens.

A second, quieter failure is that the process itself frequently causes harm. People are named before anything has been established. Evidence is reviewed one-sidedly. Neither the person raising a concern nor the person named has reliable protection. This proposal addresses both failures: it defines an adjudication structure that is independent by construction, and it constrains that structure so it cannot become the harm it exists to address.

## Specification

### Authority boundaries

The adjudication body claims exactly one of four questions.

| Question | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Was there an ethics violation? | The adjudication body |
| Was there a crime? | The state |
| Can this person hold a professional license? | The relevant licensing board |
| Can this person remain in this community? | The community |

The body issues findings and may recommend. It does not impose consequences, cannot compel action, and holds no enforcement power. This is a design choice, not a limitation to be engineered away. A body that purports to end someone's livelihood invites liability it cannot carry and authority it has not earned.

What a community does with a finding is the community's decision. Whether it acted is recorded.

### The category gate

Categorization is the first act of any process and determines everything that follows.

**Category 1 — Interpersonal Rupture (non-physical).** Emotional and relational. No physical contact or physical boundary violation is alleged. Requires both parties to participate. If the person who raised the concern declines to participate, the person named is fully exonerated — no finding, no record held against them, no disclosure. Repair then becomes a private, voluntary matter if the person named chooses to pursue it.

**Category 2 — Ethical Violation (physical component alleged).** May proceed even where the person harmed declines to participate. Someone who experienced physical harm may be traumatised, may fear retaliation, or may be unable to face the person who harmed them, and should never be required to do so as a condition of accountability. Evidence may be drawn from anonymous reports, corroborating accounts, pattern analysis, and the respondent's own account.

#### Category concerns what is alleged, not what is proven

This distinction is load-bearing. A described physical component makes a concern Category 2 on the day it is filed, regardless of whether it is later substantiated. If category depended on proof, the body would need evidence to categorize and categorization to gather evidence — a deadlock.

"They touched me during the session" is Category 2 immediately, and remains Category 2 even if the review concludes it did not occur. The finding changes; the category does not.

What the finding determines is the ethical question: what the contact was, whether it was consented to, whether it was discussed beforehand, whether it fell within the scope of the container.

#### Level 3: genuinely ambiguous concerns

A concern enters Level 3 only where it is too ambiguous as filed to determine whether a physical component is being alleged. "He was inappropriate with me" requires clarification. A concern describing contact does not.

**Category must be assigned by day 30.** If the body cannot establish by then that a physical component was alleged, the concern defaults to Category 1 — not as a convenience, but because the absence of an alleged physical component is the definition of Category 1.

**During Level 3, Category 2 rules apply.** The respondent receives Category 2 protections: no naming, no consequences, no status change. The body may proceed on available evidence. The 1:1 dialogue requirement does not apply and activates only if the concern resolves to Category 1. Defaulting to the more protective posture is correct when nothing has been established.

#### Re-categorization guard

Either party may challenge the categorization. Under the pathway rules above, this creates an exploitable move: a Category 2 case proceeding without the complainant could be challenged down to Category 1, where non-participation triggers exoneration.

**Re-categorization from 2 to 1 requires that the physical component be affirmatively absent from the record, not merely unproven.** "The evidence did not establish it" is not equivalent to "the concern never described it," and only the second permits reclassification. Any category change requires a documented affirmative basis, recorded in the registry.

### Severity ladder and clock

Category determines the pathway. Severity determines resourcing and timeline. They are orthogonal.

| Level | Meaning | Target from date raised |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category 1, both parties willing, straightforward facilitation | 5 business days |
| 2 | Category 1 with contested elements or complex facilitation | 15 business days |
| 3 | Ambiguous as filed; category must be assigned | 30 business days |
| 4 | Category 2 confirmed; full evidence review and deliberation | 45 business days |
| 5 | Category 2 with an adjacent criminal component | 60 business days |

**The clock starts when the concern is first raised with the community** and runs cumulatively. It measures to the **finding**, not to community action, because the body does not control what a community does next.

**Severity is provisional at intake**, assigned for resourcing only. It firms up as the case develops and is finalized at closure. Where severity changes, the revised timeline is committed in writing to both people.

**Severity may fall as well as rise**, with documented reasoning. The clock never shortens; a timeline is not compressed retroactively. Downward moves are logged as prominently as upward ones, because the rate at which initial reads turn out wrong is a direct measure of the body's calibration.

**Escalation to a new level triggers a re-committed date.** Time spent at each level is recorded, which is what reveals where cases actually stall.

**Passing a target triggers an automatic escalation review.** This is not punitive. It is a required account of what is blocking resolution and when it will conclude, committed in writing to both people. Justice delayed is justice denied, and that applies here even though this is not a legal process. People inside a case cannot move forward with their lives while it is unresolved.

### Criminal scope

Level 5 does not mean the body adjudicates a crime. It means an ethics case exists alongside a criminal matter, which is harder because the respondent will likely be silent on counsel's advice and the ethical questions are more contested.

The body determines whether a criminal component appears to exist and notifies both parties that **it is resolving the ethics question only**. Both parties accept that scope, or they take the matter out of the ethics system and handle the criminal question themselves. The body is not adjudicating, and is not liable for, the criminal matter.

The scope agreement is narrow and must be worded precisely: it means neither party may demand the body adjudicate the criminal question. It is not, and must never read as, any agreement not to report a crime. Mandatory reporting duties that attach to body members override any participation agreement.

**If a party exits to pursue the criminal matter,** the ethics case continues under Category 2 rules. The exit is recorded as its own outcome rather than as abandonment.

**Where a respondent declines to submit evidence under advice of counsel,** this is recorded distinctly from a refusal to engage. Both are non-participation; only one says something about the person.

### Licensure scope

The body adjudicates the community-ethics question regardless of whether a party holds a professional license. A licensing board handles licensure. The two run in parallel and are not exclusive.

If a community decides to remove someone from membership, whether that person retains a license is irrelevant to the community's decision. Communities control membership; boards control licensure.

### Scope of the system

**Within scope:** concerns between seekers and providers, or between community members, involving interpersonal ruptures or ethical violations. This includes boundary violations, deception, abuse of relational trust, miscommunication causing genuine harm, and conduct inconsistent with stated community values.

**Referred out:** anything involving minors; anything involving an ongoing danger to a specific person requiring immediate intervention. Referrals are recorded in the registry so a community can see that a serious concern was raised and handled responsibly without learning anything about the people involved.

Criminal conduct is not referred out of the ethics question. It is referred out of the *criminal* question, which the body never held. The ethics case continues in its own lane.

### Disclosure tiers

| Tier | Who sees it | What they see |
|---|---|---|
| Working layer | Adjudication body only | Full identified record, evidence, communications. Access-controlled and encrypted. Destroyed or sealed at closure per the retention policy. |
| Community layer | The community where the concern arose | That a case was raised, that it was reviewed by the independent body, what the finding was, what was recommended. No names. |
| Public registry | Anyone | De-identified structured record per ECP-2; aggregate patterns; body performance metrics. |

The body does not name anyone at the community tier. Identities are facts a community already holds or does not; the body neither supplies nor confirms them. Its role is to render and report the finding.

Reporting back to the community is a **term of participation**, agreed before any case begins, and includes recording whether the community acted.

Individual identity is disclosed only where a Category 2 finding concludes there is an ongoing safety risk, and only after both people have been told disclosure is coming.

### The adjudication body

#### Composition

Members are **not active practitioners** in the psychedelic or mental health space. This is the structural requirement that creates independence. The peer review problem — practitioners protecting people they know and depend on — cannot be solved by asking practitioners to try harder.

Candidates come from adjacent fields: restorative justice practitioners, retired healthcare ombudspersons, trauma-informed mediators with community accountability experience, ethics practitioners from journalism, social work, or education.

**At least 51% must have direct personal experience with psychedelic states.** Someone without it may understand process fairness but will struggle with the vulnerability, the intensity of the therapeutic bond in an altered state, and why harm in that context lands differently. Independence from the field's professional and financial networks is essential; experiential understanding of the territory is equally essential. Both must be present at all times.

The body is an odd number to break ties.

#### Conflict of interest

Before accepting any case, each member confirms they do not know either person, hold no financial relationship with any organization either person is affiliated with, and have no prior involvement with the concern. Any conflict requires recusal from that case.

Personal relationships are distinguished from professional ones, and the degree of acquaintance is disclosed rather than answered yes or no. Knowing one party better than the other colors adjudication even where no member intends it.

#### Operation

Decisions are collective and issued under Chatham House Rules. The body speaks as a body. This protects members from retaliation in a field where reputations travel quickly, while keeping decision-making accountable through collective performance transparency.

Reasoning is documented for every decision. It remains in the working layer, available to both people if they have grounds for a process appeal, and is the source of the structured reasoning field published in the registry.

#### Compensation

Funding mechanism is TBD.

**No speed bonuses** — they corrupt judgment by creating pressure to classify low, skip difficult deliberation, or avoid Level 4 and 5 cases.

Timeliness pressure comes from published aggregate performance: average days to finding by severity, percentage within target, open case count, appeals rate. A body that is consistently late, or that loses appeals on process grounds, loses its mandate. Reputation is the accountability mechanism.

#### Terms and recusal

Members are elected by dues-paying participating community members from a pool of candidates meeting the independence and expertise requirements. Terms are one year, rotational with staggered expiry. No member serves more than two consecutive terms.

If a concern is raised involving a current member, that member is immediately recused from all active cases pending outcome, and the case is handled by remaining members plus a temporary substitute from the candidate pool.

### Appeals

Appeals address **documented process failures only**: a standard not followed, a conflict not disclosed and recused, notification not simultaneous, or a severity or category assignment the documented rationale cannot support.

An accepted appeal reassigns the case to different members and reruns the process from the point of failure. A rejected appeal leaves the original finding standing.

Outcome disagreement is not grounds for appeal. Either person may note dissatisfaction in the permanent record. That notation is preserved and does not reopen the case.

### Amendment of this specification

Amendment follows ECP-0: fourteen-day Last Call, 60% supermajority of participating voters, in-place revision with a version increment and changelog entry.

Where an amendment is informed by a real case, the proposal references that case only by its registry record identifier, describing behaviors in anonymized terms. Names may never be used.

### Certification separation

A community that adopts this standard, receives a finding, and does nothing has broken its own stated commitment. No one can compel it to act. But conformance certification can be declined.

This works only because certification and adjudication are separate entities. One guard is essential: **certification turns on whether a community followed its adopted process, never on whether it reached the outcome the certifier would have preferred.** Otherwise certification becomes a backdoor for imposing outcomes and the independence collapses.

### Transfer charter

The registry and this standard are intended for an independent steward with no commercial interests, with Mind Lumen as a founding member holding no veto over categorization criteria or the taxonomy.

Transfer occurs when defined criteria are met — a threshold number of participating communities and adjudicated cases. Until then Mind Lumen hosts and stewards, and states publicly that it intends to hand over. A standard nobody has used is a document; a standard that resolved cases fairly and transparently is a model, and only the second is worth a foundation.

### Legal dependencies

These must be resolved by counsel before the framework is offered to communities. They are not questions a specification can settle by asserting an answer.

1. **Participation agreement — criminal scope.** Precise wording, including that it creates no obligation not to report a crime, and carve-outs for mandatory reporting duties attaching to body members.
2. **Participation agreement — community-tier reporting.** Consent to findings being reported back, and to action or inaction being recorded.
3. **Data Contributor Agreement.** Inbound terms for contributed case data, including confirmation that original participants' terms permit secondary use of de-identified content.
4. **Right to erasure.** Formal opinion on whether the de-identified public registry constitutes personal data under GDPR and similar frameworks.
5. **Insurance and entity structure.** Errors and omissions, and directors and officers coverage for body members. Holding no enforcement power reduces exposure substantially but does not eliminate it.
6. **IP assignment.** Mind Lumen is a 501(c)(3); contributions to and from the organization carry assignment implications for the open-licensed artifacts.

## Costs and who bears them

**Participating communities** bear membership dues funding the body's per-case fees, the labour of electing members, and the obligation to receive findings and have action or inaction recorded.

**Adjudication body members** bear case work at flat fees that are unlikely to match commercial mediation rates, plus the reputational exposure of serving in a contested space, partly mitigated by Chatham House Rules.

**People raising concerns** bear the cost of a process that will not always resolve as they hope, and that closes on process integrity rather than on their satisfaction.

**People concerns are raised about** bear the cost of a process running about them, including where a Category 2 case proceeds without the complainant's participation.

**The commons steward** bears infrastructure, coordination, and the recruitment of qualified independent members, which is the hardest of these to supply.

## Benefits and who they accrue to

**People raising concerns** get a process with defined timelines, evidence taken from both sides, and a body with no financial stake in the outcome.

**People concerns are raised about** get protection from being named before anything is established, a right to challenge categorization, and — in Category 1 non-participation — full exoneration instead of indefinite suspicion.

**Communities** get a structure that removes the impossible position of judging their own members, and evidence they can point to that accountability happens.

**The field** gets a factual baseline. The prevailing narrative about how common ethics violations are may be accurate or may be distorted by the fact that violations attract attention and healthy practice does not. Nobody knows, because nobody has been counting.

**Providers collectively** benefit from context. Where no baseline exists, a single accusation carries disproportionate weight precisely because there is nothing to compare it against.

## Alternatives considered

**Professional association peer review.** The default in most fields. Rejected because practitioners judging peers they know and financially depend on is the conflict this design exists to remove, and it cannot be fixed by asking members to be more neutral.

**Regulatory or licensing bodies as first authority.** Rejected as insufficient rather than wrong. They handle licensure, slowly and punitively, and have no jurisdiction over unlicensed practitioners, underground communities, or relational harm that breaks no law. They run in parallel; they do not substitute.

**A body with enforcement power.** Considered and rejected on both liability and legitimacy grounds. A body that could end someone's livelihood invites litigation it cannot survive and claims authority no community granted it. Findings plus published inaction is weaker in the short term and more durable.

**Severity as a single axis subsuming category.** The earlier design fused them. Rejected because pathway and resourcing are different questions: category determines whether a case can proceed without the complainant, which is a factual matter answerable on day one, while severity is a complexity estimate that legitimately changes as a case develops.

**A statute of limitations on Category 2.** Rejected. Delayed disclosure of physical and sexual harm is the norm, not the exception, and most jurisdictions have been extending or eliminating limitation periods for exactly that reason. Elapsed time is recorded as an evidentiary factor instead of a bar to filing.

**Tolling the clock during parallel criminal proceedings.** Considered. Rejected in favour of explicit scope disclaimer, because tolling makes the ethics timeline hostage to a criminal one and would guarantee that every Level 5 case misses its target, rendering published performance metrics meaningless.

**All practitioners excluded from the body, with no experiential requirement.** Rejected because process fairness alone does not equip someone to understand why harm in an altered state lands differently. Hence independence from professional networks combined with a 51% experiential floor.

## Open questions

Not everything can be answered in advance, and pretending otherwise would be its own failure. These remain open and are expected to be resolved through the ECP-0 amendment process, informed by registry data as it accumulates.

1. **Emotional abuse of power without a physical component.** Manipulation, coercion, gaslighting, and exploitation of a power differential cause serious harm without physical contact. A single such concern currently falls under Category 1 and requires both parties. One possible threshold is pattern evidence — multiple independent complaints, from different people, describing similar behavior across separate incidents at different times. Multiple complaints about a single incident are explicitly not a pattern; the volume reflects the reach of one event, not a course of conduct. The independence test and the count threshold are both unresolved.
2. **Pattern cases have no clock.** Pattern detection requires other cases to accumulate, which can take years and cannot live inside a 30-day Level 3. The proposed handling is that the individual case closes under Category 1 rules with a persistent pattern flag, and a new pattern case opens if flags accumulate against the same respondent — which requires an identity linkage the registry deliberately does not hold. How this works without reintroducing identity is open.
3. **Community inaction.** With no enforcement power, transparency is the only lever, and it is slow. Whether reporting findings back plus recording inaction is sufficient is an empirical question the registry will answer.
4. **Findings do not travel.** Because the registry is de-identified, someone removed from one community can join another with no way for the second to know. This is the correct privacy tradeoff and a real limitation. Portable findings would require a separate consent-based mechanism with different privacy rules.
5. **Findings against community leadership.** Where the subject of a finding is a leader or board member, the reporting path must reach the membership rather than route through the people concerned.

*This is a working draft, offered for open debate. Feedback, adaptation, and forks are welcome. Attribution is requested; conformance claims require the mark.*
